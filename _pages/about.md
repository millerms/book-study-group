Three friends got curious about Buddhism and decided we would understand it better if we stopped reading around at random and made ourselves a plan.

The idea is to see the broad historical shape, spend some time with primary texts, and notice where later traditions agree, disagree, or ask different questions. We want enough structure to keep moving, but not so much that we accidentally enroll ourselves in an imaginary seminar.

## The Rough Plan

We begin with the world of the Buddha and some early teachings preserved in the Pāli Canon. From there we follow the history through early schools, Theravada, Mahayana, Madhyamaka, East Asian Buddhism, Vajrayana, and modern Buddhism.

We are not attempting to read the entire Pāli Canon. Everyone can relax.

## A Few Things We’re Trying to Remember

- Start with what the text actually says.
- Ask what was happening historically, and be honest when the answer is uncertain.
- Notice how later Buddhist traditions have understood the same material.
- Watch for modern assumptions we may be reading backward into the text.
- When a text proposes a practice, see whether trying it makes the idea any clearer.

Those are useful differences, but they are habits rather than rules. History, tradition, and personal experience can all tell us something. We just want to notice which kind of conversation we’re having.

## When We Meet

We’ll read the main text beforehand when possible, bring whatever stood out or did not make sense, and talk it through. Passing is fine. Confusion is expected. Three friends reading a few suttas is not an ecumenical council.
