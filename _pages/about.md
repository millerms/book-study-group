This is a small, informal study group for people who want to understand Buddhism historically, textually, and practically. The curriculum is intellectually serious but designed for readers who do not have specialist training.

## What We Are Trying to Understand

1. How Buddhism developed historically and how its major traditions and lineages took shape.
2. What early Buddhist texts say, especially texts preserved in the Pāli Canon.
3. How later traditions developed, including Theravada, Mahayana, Madhyamaka, East Asian Buddhism, Vajrayana, and modern Western Buddhism.
4. How historical inquiry, inherited interpretation, and personal investigation ask different questions of the same material.

## Three Lenses

Throughout the course, we distinguish among three kinds of claim:

**Historical reconstruction** asks what we can reasonably infer happened, when a source or practice emerged, and how confident we should be.

**Traditional interpretation** asks how Buddhist communities and lineages have understood a teaching and what role it plays within a living tradition.

**Personal or contemporary practice** asks what happens when we investigate these ideas and practices ourselves, in our own circumstances.

None of these automatically invalidates the others. Problems usually begin when we collapse them together without noticing.

## A Typical Week

Each weekly guide will eventually include:

- a historical orientation;
- core and optional primary readings;
- secondary background reading;
- key concepts and historical context;
- discussion questions; and
- practice or reflection questions when appropriate.

The detailed reading lists are intentionally not filled in yet. They will be developed and verified separately, with links to freely available texts whenever possible.
